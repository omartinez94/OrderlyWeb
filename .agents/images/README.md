## Recommended follow-ups

These are placeholders meant to validate layout, color, and tone. Before shipping:

1. **Commission a real set** in the same style — AI generations won't survive close inspection. A consistent hand-drawn or commissioned vector set is the long-term answer.
2. **Add real product screenshots** to replace `03-mockup-*` once the UI is stable.
3. **Add customer logos** to replace `03-logo-strip.png` once partnerships exist.
4. **Localize** the people illustrations — the current team illustration isn't specific to any region.
5. **Build a style sheet** (line weight, color, corner radius, shadow style) to lock in visual cohesion before the next pass.
6. **Re-export at 2K** for `01-*`, `02-*`, `04-*`, `05-*`, `06-*` if any will be used at large display sizes (KDS tablets, conference slides).

## Style notes for the r1 direction

- The semi-realistic approach gives the marketing surfaces a more "grown-up" feel, closer to Stripe/Linear/Vercel marketing illustrations than flat SaaS UI art.
- For the people-bearing images (`03-team`, `05-staff-avatar-set`, `04-staff-management`), faces will be the weakest spot — AI photorealism tends to uncanny. Worth verifying those individually before locking them in.
- The non-people assets (objects, surfaces, food, equipment) read very well at this style and should hold up to closer inspection.
- The two empty/absent-thing illustrations (`01-no-staff-yet`, `01-no-menu-items`) lean more on object photography than illustration — that reads professional and is the right call for the use case.
